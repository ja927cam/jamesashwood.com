01

Real-time games in OCaml

Can OCaml be a genuinely good stack for real-time multiplayer games, on both the server and the client? The backend side is promising — typed binary protocols, a single authoritative writer, predictable allocation — and One Million Chessboards is a useful test of that. The client is the open question: is js_of_ocaml (or Brr/WASM) good enough for a real-time canvas game, or does the JavaScript boundary and tooling push you back to TypeScript? I'd like to build the same small game twice and compare latency, bundle size, debugging and how much code is actually shared.

02

OSM for severe-weather risk

Weather models are good at predicting the hazard and much worse at predicting the impact. A rainfall forecast does not know which underpass floods, which road becomes impassable, or which care home sits in the low-lying part of town. OpenStreetMap has a lot of the missing exposure data — buildings, roads, waterways, land use, bridges — but it is uneven, incomplete and often out of date. Can we combine forecast fields with OSM features and simple hydrological or network models to produce a useful, uncertainty-aware danger map? And where is OSM simply not good enough?

03

Small local models, better reasoning

How much of "reasoning" is the model, and how much is the harness around it? Small local models are cheap and private, but they fail on multi-step programming problems in ways that look structural: they stop planning, lose state, or repair the wrong thing. I want to know whether better decomposition, execution feedback, verifiers and tighter token budgets can close a meaningful part of the gap — and whether the answer is a custom harness rather than a better prompt.
